Research Identity

Evolutionary history, made measurable

Our work asks which evolutionary patterns repeat across lineages, when a molecular signal is statistically resolvable, and how sequence-level evidence can be connected to biological mechanism without overstating inference.

Research

Current Research Directions

The group connects evolutionary questions with method development, explicit uncertainty, and reproducible analysis across biological scales.

01

Evolutionary Replay after Genome Duplication

Testing whether ancestral gene-lineage identity predicts duplicate retention across independent whole-genome duplications, and where event-specific history modifies replay.

Latest preprint
02

Selection as a Measurement Problem

Investigating when episodic branch-site signals are identifiable from finite alignments using simulation, calibration, likelihood-free inference, and guarded reporting.

03

Context-Aware Sequence Inference

Developing learned residue-level scoring for multiple sequence alignment and reproducible workflows that expose sensitivity to alignment and trimming choices.

Evidence-Aware Biological Omics
